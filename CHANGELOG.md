The reconcile planned every guarded step again from the first one in the chat, and each came out as it was stored, because an edit takes nothing back by itself: Re-read is how an edit reaches the story. The stored records differ only in bookkeeping hashes: the boundary and checkpoint hashes after the edited reply are stamped again by the next swipe, delete or Re-read, not by the edit itself. <details><summary>Full notes</summary>

**The world moves on its own, and the Director directs.** Everything in this list is behind one switch, **Settings › World and time › World events** (off by default). With it off the world features below do nothing and the story runs as in 0.1.0; the few changes that are not behind the switch are listed under "Also changed with the switch off".

- **World events and conditions.** A world pack can describe what may happen in its world: one-off and recurring events, with chances, seasons, what they need and what ends them. Named conditions of the world ("grain is dear", "the roads are closed") hold at a place, a route, a region or a realm and fade after their time. The story can set and lift them too.
- **The daily world step.** When the story crosses into a new day, the world takes its step: events may happen, conditions fade, people of the pack move about. A jump of several days steps each day. **Tempo** sets how fast the world moves.
- **News travels.** News of a happening reaches each place after the time a courier needs along the routes. It is fact as far as its reach goes, and one step beyond that it arrives as rumour, naming only the region. The reply's tail carries a short **[World now]** note: the conditions where the scene is and the newest news that has reached it.
- **Open templates: the Director fills small happenings.** A pack's small templates can leave the details open; the Director fills them in from the pack's own menu, and the pack's fallback text is used when that call fails.
- **The Director judge and the arc world note.** When the arc planner makes a plan, one more short call checks its beats against the world (the events under way, the protected people, the hidden lore) and accepts, reshapes or refuses each one. The same call writes a short note on how the arc and the world meet, shown under the arc line. The planner reacts to world events but never decides them.
- **NPC State keeps a world pack's secrets until they are earned.** A pack person's secrets wait in their dossier and come out only when their condition is met.
- **The World tab and Settings rows.** The World tab shows the conditions where you are and how the news came. Settings › World and time has **World events**, **Tempo** and **Director calls this game year** (read only).
- **Sample world pack: Ersveil.** `examples/packs/ersveil-world` (and `examples/packs/ersveil-world.zip`) is a full world pack: 31 people (12 companions with portraits), lore, realms, powers, conditions and 103 world events. Its world events run only with World events on.
- **Known limit:** a world pack's cast fields beyond the basic ones do not reach NPC State yet. Pack format 4 fixes it.

**Also changed with the switch off.**

- A pack may carry the world-pack sections (conditions, lore, regions, realms, world events, templates and the rest) and installs; before, they made the install fail.
- The World tab shows a region's name instead of its id when the pack names its regions (display only).
- The day digest no longer lists the names check's "Name: … is new" lines as the day's events (LAB2-5); the meeting stays in the Journal's Met.
- Settings › World and time shows the World events row.
- The fixes and speed-ups merged since 0.1.0 (below) are not behind the switch.

**Fixes and speed-ups since 0.1.0.**

- **Follow star reaches the narrator; People › Elsewhere shows what followed people are doing (G3).** The star (stored per chat by `people.follow`, shown on the People tile, the dossier and the Now Here card, set only in the dossier as in the mock) now also puts the person on the narrator's key list: Follow sends `keypeople.pin { followed: true }`, Unfollow `keypeople.unpin`, so the away lines, the tail and the death/archive sync work as for Key people with no change to the model's wording. A follow-made pin does not count against the Key people limit (a pin from the World tab still does). If the key list refuses or has no such person, the star stands. People followed before this build are not pinned until followed again. An away person's Elsewhere tile adds `back <date>` to the place line and shows what they are doing in the small italic line under it (the mock has no such line there, so it is the mock's `said` style). Key people itself is still there (removed in B4-9).
- **Two fixes from the second live A/B (LAB2-4, LAB2-6).** (1) A repaired day jump keeps the time of day the reply gives: the repair prompt now says that after a stretch of a day or more, when the reply gives a time of day ("after dusk"), the clock time goes on the next line (`! time +10d` then `~ time 7:00 pm`); the safety net already accepts both lines. (2) The `[Cast]` note for a first meeting no longer gives the narrator the stranger's name: it names them by a descriptive alias ("the ferrywoman", else "a stranger"), leaves name forms of the name out, says the player does not know the name until it is said in the story, and the name, or any word of it (first or family name, 3+ letters), is also replaced in the note's own text, whatever the aliases are. Someone the story already named (met in the story) keeps the name as before. The world pulse's chance-meeting line ("World this reply: … crosses your path in <place>, by chance.") does the same: it calls the unmet person by their first descriptive alias ("The ferrywoman crosses your path…"), else "A stranger crosses your path…", never by name (the line only ever picks people not yet met, so it never names anyone).
- **Keep the last two replies' change blocks in the prompt (on by default).** A switch on Settings › Models › Calls, "Keep the last two replies’ change blocks in the prompt" (host setting `keepRecentBlocks`; Reset Models turns it back on). The two newest replies reach the model with the `<Changes>` block the narrator wrote for them, so it sees its own recent blocks; older replies lose theirs as before. Only the narrator's own lines come back (never repair or Assist lines, nor a block the reply was cut off in); a reply without a block counts as one of the two. In one live round the narrator kept its own block on 9 of 10 replies (4 of 10 and 5 of 10 before), at about 390 tokens more per request and no extra call. Two guards go with it: a `+ item` or `+ coin` line a reply copies from those two blocks (identical in every part) is not applied again unless its story, after its first paragraph, shows it happening again; a line that differs at all always stays (a Re-read judges it the same way), and a reply that ends in a `<Changes>` block left open with no line in it now counts as having no block, so the repair is asked (it was skipped before). Off, every prompt and every capture is exactly as before.
- **Less work per reply in the new view (perf batch PB2).** Nothing drawn changes; the view reads less. (1) The tab strip's flags (the RPG is on, a fight is open or waiting, an interaction is open and its title) come from a light `flags` view (`ui/select/build.js`), not from building the whole Now card and the fight and scene views on every engine event; a port without a `flags` view is read the old way. (2) The flags, the card, the open tab and the chat surfaces (the line under the reply, the drawer, the reply-menu button) are each read at most once a frame, however many events a reply sends: the flags are a redraw section drawn first in its frame, so a fight's tab is in the strip before the tab draws, and the chat surfaces are drawn by one per-frame callback (`frameCoalesced` in `ui/runtime/redraw.js`) on the same port and SillyTavern events as before. (3) While the suite is closed its card and tabs are not read; opening it draws what changed meanwhile (`isOpen` and `reveal()` on the redraw scheduler). The card's sky is painted from the same card read. Measured in the perf rig on the 18 Nov A/B chat (11 people), per reply: with the World tab open, card builds 14 → 2, chat builds 12 → 2, fight and scene reads 30 → 0, `npc.cast` 26 → 4; with the suite closed, card builds 14 → 0 and chat builds 12 → 2.
- **No 5-second wait after an edit or a delete (perf PB5).** NPC State no longer polls for 5 s after each edit or delete of a message, and the console warning "edit event did not settle … within 5000ms" (or "delete event …") is gone. SillyTavern sends the edit and delete events after it has changed the chat, so the wait never saw a change and timed out every time, while each of its polls (every 50 ms) built two full copies of NPC State's branch history. Now the chat key and the swipe state are read directly, and the wait takes one look on the next tick and ends. The edit or delete is still read again twice, as before: by NPC State's immediate reconcile and by its queued branch reconcile. A second edit or delete in the same tick still settles against the first one's baseline. One that comes later is now read on its own: it no longer adds a third, "settled" reconcile, and its immediate reconcile no longer takes the first event's earlier message number or delete. While the store holds the people (once Story Suite has accepted NPC State) every one of these reconciles ends at the store-owned exit, so nothing changes there; the old sidecar path (NPC State refused by the host) was not measured.
- **Three fixes from the live A/B (LAB-2, LAB-3, LAB-4).** (1) The repair no longer overshoots a travel stretch: its prompt tells the model to count only the time the reply tells, and a repaired `! time` stretch over a day is cut to `! time +24h` (with a Problems note) unless the reply names a stretch longer than a day ("the next morning", "two days later", "after three days of hard riding", "for a week", "after a fortnight", "on the third day"; a distance still to cover or "the second day" does not count). The cut line is what is stored, so Re-read and reload read the same. (2) When a model drafts the reply in the message itself (its first paragraphs, a review, then the whole reply again), the draft in front of the second copy is cut and a Problems note says so. Only a real doubled reply is cut: the whole first paragraph must come back ending a paragraph, and what follows must repeat most of the text before it, so a refrain or a flashback that reopens on the opening words keeps its text; a first paragraph under 60 characters, and a Continue, are never touched. <details><summary>Full notes</summary>

First release of Story Suite as one extension. It brings together what was Story Block, Story Director and NPC State Delta (now called NPC State). Their own changelogs, with every earlier version, are kept in `docs/history/packages/`.

This part is for players: what is new, what is different, what is gone. The notes for developers are under "For developers" below. It starts fresh: nothing from the three old extensions is carried over (no settings, chats or sidecar files), so read the install steps in `docs/INSTALL.md` before you update.

### One extension

- Story Block, Story Director and NPC State Delta are now one extension, Story Suite. You install one thing, there is one drawer in Extensions (headed `Story Suite 0.1.0`), and NPC State's settings are a section of it. Updating later is the Update button in Extensions → Manage extensions.
- Delete the old three first (disabling is not enough). The new extension does not start while the old Story Block is still on, and it says so.
- Settings are in one place, with a Models page that lists every model call (the story, quick helpers the reply waits for, background work, the safety net that reads changes a reply forgot) and the connection each one uses. One thing moved: the safety net now runs on the Background connection; it used to run on Quick helpers, so check the two if you set them differently.
- If one part fails to start, the rest still runs; Settings → Diagnostics lists it.

### The new view

- The new view is the one you see. It has the tabs Now, Story, People, Party, Bag, World and Fight, one Settings overlay, and the same look in dark and light. The old view stays only as a hidden fallback (Settings → Tools → Developer tools) until it is removed.
- Each reply's menu and the line under it list what that reply changed (items, coin, a move, who came or left).
- Settings has a page for each part: Start, Models, Director, RPG, World and time, Money, Chat extras, People, Packs and trackers (install, switch off or remove a pack; read one from the character's lorebook), Add-ons and Tools. The switches on the module cards work, and "Reset to Standard" redraws the open page.
- Story → Trackers works: pin a tracker to Now, change its values by hand, remove one this chat made, or add one of three starters.

- Gone from the old view, on purpose: save points (to go back, restart from a message), the layout editor (Customize) and the strip across the top of the screen.

### Combat Rules v1

- There is one set of combat rules and one way to fight. Every fight is played by code on the fight board: you choose for each party member, the dice are rolled by code, and the story is told afterwards. The older rules, the roll planner and narrated fights are removed, and so are the Rolls and Fight board switches.
- A character has seven stats from 1 to 30 (STR, VIT, AGI, DEX, INT, WIS, CHA), and HP, MP and SP worked out from the stats and the level. Luck is gone: WIS covers luck, will and senses, and a character file or pack that still says LUK is read as WIS. Skills have a type (attack, area, defence, evasion, movement, heal, support, utility) and practice from 0 to 600.
- The New character wizard makes you (the name is your persona's) and has no "Who they are" field. A companion joins only through the join card: Add to party, or Edit first.
- A foe group a lost or fled fight left starts the next fight from the same HP, if the story sets the same group again. Foes are set with a tier from mob to apex; the fight set-up asks for it and follows a size rule (an even fight is at most 4 standard foes).
- Story checks (`? roll`) are success or fail, with a natural 20 decisive when it meets the DC.
- The built-in skill sets (Guild fighter, River scout, Hedge mage, Field medic, Silver tongue) carry typed skills.
- A chat that already holds a character made under the earlier rules is not converted; start a new chat. (Fresh start applies here too.)

### NPC State: lines and Refresh

- NPC State no longer asks the narrator for a hidden block of JSON. Changes to people are short lines in the reply: `+ npc` for a new person (at most four a reply, and one fill-in after the reply writes the rest), `~ npc` for a change, `~ tie` for a relationship (kin, partner, friend, ally, mentor, serves, rival, enemy, owes), `level:` when the story states a level. This makes replies shorter and the changes visible on the reply card.
- A death is now recorded by NPC State, and the narrator is no longer taught `- key Name | died`.
- The people list the narrator sees names active people only; archived, dead and long-unseen people appear only when your message names them.
- Switched on by default: the one-pass way and the rolling Refresh are on. A profile saved before this release is switched on once; after that your own choice stands.
- Refresh (the quiet catch-up that fills in slow fields: personality, speech, background, mannerisms and the rest) is rolling. Each person waits N replies after their last Refresh (setting "One-pass catch-up", default 4, 0 = off), and each reply refreshes up to K people (new setting "NPCs refreshed per reply", 1 to 3, default 1), longest-waiting first, most-seen next. Your own Refresh button counts. It can read messages SillyTavern has hidden (for example by a memory extension), but not swiped-away or deleted replies.
- A person's ⋯ → Refresh in the dossier now runs a catch-up for that person (before, it did nothing).
- Quotes in the dossier show as `#12`, the message number SillyTavern shows.

### Replies that forget their change block

- Every reply is asked to end with a `<Changes>` block, empty when nothing changed, so a missing block means something was forgotten.
- Repair is on by default (Settings → Models → Calls, "Repair a reply's missing changes"). When a reply has no block, one quick call reads the reply and writes the changes it plainly shows: items, coin, a move, a quest or lead, the time. It never writes experience, loot, rewards, heals or rolls.
- The repaired lines are marked "Assist" on the reply's sheet. **Discard repair** on the sheet takes the repair out and the reply is not repaired again. Swipes, going back, deleting a reply in the middle, rewinding, reloading and Re-read all keep what was stored or discarded.
- The repair does not list a lead it already sees as open; it moves the clock when the reply's actions take time, even with no clock time in the text; and a `<Changes>` tag that is only mentioned in the text, inside code marks, is not read as a block.
- If a reply ends up with no recorded changes, the Problems list says so and the new view shows "No changes recorded — Re-read" under the latest reply. The Problems list is kept with the chat and comes back after a reload.

### Known limits

- How often the narrator writes the change block depends on the model; the repair fills the gaps but is a guess from the reply, so check the changes card when it matters.
- Discard repair works only on the latest reply.
- The "No changes recorded" badge is on the latest reply only.

### Activities

- An open interaction (a fight, a mini-game or any module's activity) gets a tab named after it, shown only while it is open. There is no general "Scene" tab any more; the word Scene stays for the Here card and the Scene sheet.

### Packs, format 3

- World packs use format 3. A pack names the Suite version it needs with `requires.suite`; a pack whose format is newer than 3 is refused.
- A pack can list its foes by tier (name, tier from mob to apex, optional level and notes); a foe it does not list takes the tier the fight set-up gives it.
- A pack can no longer tune combat numbers: the numbers are fixed by Combat Rules v1 (a pack that sets them loads, with a warning, and the numbers are ignored).
- The example world Ersveil is converted to Combat Rules v1 only as far as needed to play (skills have explicit types; its two luck items use WIS). Its story is not rewritten. The small Riverside starter is no longer shipped as an example; it lives on as a test file.

### Removed

- The older combat rules, the roll planner, narrated and manual fights, the Rolls and Fight board settings.
- The hidden JSON block of NPC State, and `- key Name | died` for the narrator.
- Save points, the layout editor and the top strip in the new view.
- The separate NPC State Delta drawer, and any migration from the three old extensions.
- The Riverside starter pack as a shipped example.
